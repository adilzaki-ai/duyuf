# Duyuf — Hajj Ops MVP

## Slide 1 — Title
Duyuf
Hajj Ops MVP

A smarter arrival and pilgrim support system for GACA and airport operations

From flight manifest to live response — before pilgrims reach the terminal

---

## Slide 2 — Why now

Hajj is one of the most operationally dense events in the world.

The real issue is not a lack of data. It is a lack of time to act on it.

- Pilgrim arrivals are uneven, unpredictable, and time-critical
- Staffing and equipment are often assigned after demand appears
- Pilgrims arrive with different mobility, language, medical, and transport needs
- Existing systems manage parts of the journey, but not the live coordination layer

Result: a missed decision window creates delay, risk, and pressure on operations teams.

---

## Slide 3 — The problem

### Today’s pain points
- Flights arrive in waves, but resource planning is still reactive
- Wheelchairs, interpreters, immigration lanes, and buses are assigned late
- Vulnerable pilgrims are not always visible before arrival
- Leaders cannot answer live operational questions in Arabic or English
- Incident response starts after a problem has already escalated

### Consequences
- Longer queues and slower movement through immigration and transfer
- Misallocated resources and avoidable bottlenecks
- Slower response to elderly, sick, or lost pilgrims
- Lower confidence in operational readiness and control

---

## Slide 4 — Strategic opportunity

### Existing systems are valuable, but fragmented
- Nusuk and digital travel workflows
- Makkah Route clearance before departure
- Smart cards and crowd monitoring
- Airport and immigration systems

### What is missing
- A live arrival orchestration layer
- Predictive staffing and equipment planning before landing
- One operational view of who is arriving, with what needs, and when
- A trusted AI analyst connected to live data
- A voice-first support path for pilgrims who do not rely on smartphone apps

We are not replacing the current system. We are connecting it into one decision layer.

---

## Slide 5 — Product vision

### Duyuf is the command-and-control layer for Hajj operations

It helps airport teams answer four questions:
1. Who is arriving, and when?
2. What support do they need?
3. Are we staffed and equipped for it?
4. What should we do next?

### Value created
- Better readiness
- Faster decisions
- Safer movement
- Reduced congestion
- More operational confidence

---

## Slide 6 — MVP scope

### 1) Arrival Wave Orchestrator
- Forecast arrivals over the next 48 hours
- Map passenger totals by flight, age, language, mobility, and medical needs
- Identify capacity gaps in immigration, buses, wheelchairs, and baggage handling
- Recommend actions before landing

### 2) Arabic / English AI Analyst
- Answer natural-language operational questions
- Work from live data, not guesswork
- Produce charts, summaries, and recommended actions

### 3) Pilgrim Incident Feed
- Send live pilgrim issues to the airport operations center
- Support dispatch and resolution workflows
- Speed up response for high-risk situations

### 4) Rafeeq Pilot
- Voice-first support for six languages: English, Arabic, Indonesian, Urdu, Turkish, French
- SOS and lost-pilgrim flows
- Built for basic phones and low-data situations

---

## Slide 7 — Airport operations console

### What the system shows
- 48-hour arrival board
- Flight-by-flight brief before wheels down
- Demand versus capacity by airport and time window
- What-if simulation for delay, added counters, buses, or wheelchairs
- Ranked action list for staffing and equipment decisions

### Example output
“Flight SV-xxxx: 41 pilgrims over 75, 12 wheelchairs, mostly Urdu speakers, diabetic meals needed.”

### Outcome
Operations teams act before the queue forms, instead of after it appears.

---

## Slide 8 — Rafeeq: a voice-first pilgrim companion

### Purpose
Support elderly, low-literacy, or non-app users who need quick help in stressful moments.

### Pilot experience
- Phone call or basic-device flow
- Large SOS and “I’m lost” buttons
- Spoken safety guidance and route support
- Escalation to a supervisor when needed
- Language coverage: English, Arabic, Indonesian, Urdu, Turkish, French

### Why it matters
- Lower barrier to help-seeking
- Faster support in crowded settings
- Higher trust in the journey experience

---

## Slide 9 — AI analyst and trust model

### Questions it can answer
- “How many Nigerian pilgrims over 70 land in Madinah tomorrow between 2am and 5am?”
- “Which flights are understaffed for wheelchair support?”
- “Which groups are most exposed if weather causes a disruption?”

### What the system does
- Pulls live operational data
- Converts it into summaries and insights
- Highlights gaps and recommended actions
- Keeps answers grounded in approved source data

### Trust model
- Human remains in the loop
- Recommendations are advisory, not autonomous
- Sensitive religious content stays within approved governance controls

## Slide 10 — Roadmap, KPIs, and ask

### MVP — one season
- Arrival Wave Orchestrator
- Arabic / English AI Analyst
- Pilgrim incident feed
- Rafeeq pilot in six languages

### KPIs
- % of flights with a pre-arrival resource brief before landing
- Reduction in queue time at immigration and bus handoff
- Faster response to distressed pilgrims
- Increased visibility of vulnerable pilgrims before escalation

### The ask
- Pilot sponsorship from GACA or a major airport partner
- Access to operational data and staffing inputs
- One-season validation window
- Partnership with operations, privacy, and governance stakeholders

### We will deliver
- A clearer operational picture before pilgrims land
- Better resource readiness and faster incident response
- A practical AI layer built on trusted data
- A foundation for the broader Hajj Ops platform

---

## Final slide — closing statement

Duyuf is not trying to replace existing systems.
It is the missing coordination layer that makes the rest of the Hajj operation work in real time.

For a single season, the right product is not the entire suite.
It is the system that helps teams see the wave, prepare for it, and respond before the problem becomes visible.

That is the right first product to build.
